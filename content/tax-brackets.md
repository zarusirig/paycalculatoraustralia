# Australian Tax Brackets 2025-26 — Income Tax Rates & Thresholds

Australia uses a progressive tax system — different portions of your income are taxed at different rates. You don't pay your top marginal rate on your entire salary. Here are the current rates, worked examples, and everything you need to understand how Australian income tax works.

> All rates on this page are sourced from the Australian Taxation Office (ATO) and apply for the income year 1 July 2025 to 30 June 2026.

---

## Current Tax Brackets — FY2025-26

These rates apply to Australian residents for tax purposes. They do not include the 2% [Medicare levy](/medicare-levy/).

| Taxable Income | Tax On This Income |
|---------------|-------------------|
| $0 – $18,200 | Nil |
| $18,201 – $45,000 | 16 cents for each $1 over $18,200 |
| $45,001 – $135,000 | $4,288 plus 30 cents for each $1 over $45,000 |
| $135,001 – $190,000 | $31,288 plus 37 cents for each $1 over $135,000 |
| $190,001 and over | $51,638 plus 45 cents for each $1 over $190,000 |

The tax-free threshold remains at $18,200. With the [Low Income Tax Offset (LITO)](/low-income-tax-offset/), the effective tax-free threshold increases to $22,575.

## How Marginal Tax Works — Step-by-Step Example

The most common misunderstanding about Australian tax is that your entire salary is taxed at one rate. It's not. Here's exactly how it works on an **$80,000 salary**:

| Tax Bracket | Income in This Bracket | Tax Rate | Tax Payable |
|------------|----------------------|----------|-------------|
| $0 – $18,200 | $18,200 | 0% | $0 |
| $18,201 – $45,000 | $26,800 | 16% | $4,288 |
| $45,001 – $80,000 | $35,000 | 30% | $10,500 |
| **Total** | **$80,000** | — | **$14,788** |

Your **marginal tax rate** is 30% (the rate on your highest bracket), but your **effective tax rate** is only 18.49% ($14,788 ÷ $80,000).

That's the key difference. You never pay 30% on your entire salary — only on the portion between $45,001 and $80,000.

## Tax Offsets That Reduce Your Tax

Tax offsets (also called rebates) directly reduce the amount of tax you owe. Unlike deductions, which reduce your taxable income, offsets reduce the final tax bill dollar-for-dollar.

### Low Income Tax Offset (LITO)

The LITO provides a tax reduction of up to $700 for lower-income earners:

| Taxable Income | LITO Amount |
|---------------|-------------|
| Up to $37,500 | $700 (full offset) |
| $37,501 – $45,000 | $700 minus 5 cents per $1 over $37,500 |
| $45,001 – $66,667 | $325 minus 1.5 cents per $1 over $45,000 |
| $66,668 and over | Nil |

Combined with the $18,200 tax-free threshold, the LITO means you can effectively earn up to $22,575 before paying any net income tax.

→ [Full LITO guide with calculator](/low-income-tax-offset/)

### Seniors and Pensioners Tax Offset (SAPTO)

Eligible seniors can receive an additional offset of up to $2,230 (singles), making their effective tax-free threshold significantly higher.

## How Tax Brackets Changed — Stage 3 Tax Cuts

The current brackets reflect the Stage 3 tax cuts that took effect on 1 July 2024. Here's what changed:

| What Changed | Before (FY2023-24) | After (FY2024-25 onwards) |
|-------------|--------------------|-----------------------------|
| Second bracket rate | 19% | **16%** |
| Third bracket rate | 32.5% | **30%** |
| Third bracket ceiling | $120,000 | **$135,000** |
| Fourth bracket ceiling | $180,000 | **$190,000** |

These changes mean every Australian taxpayer earning above $18,200 pays less tax than they did before July 2024. The biggest dollar savings go to middle-income earners in the $45,000–$135,000 range.

---

## Upcoming Changes — FY2026-27

From 1 July 2026, the tax rate on the second bracket ($18,201–$45,000) is legislated to drop from 16% to **15%**. A further reduction to 14% is scheduled for 1 July 2027.

This change will save:
- Someone on $45,000: an extra $268/year
- Someone on $80,000: an extra $268/year
- Someone on $200,000: an extra $268/year

(The saving is the same for everyone earning above $45,000 because only the second bracket rate changes.)

## Non-Resident Tax Rates 2025-26

If you're a non-resident for tax purposes, different rates apply. Non-residents don't get the tax-free threshold and don't pay the Medicare levy.

| Taxable Income | Tax Rate |
|---------------|----------|
| $0 – $135,000 | 30 cents for each $1 |
| $135,001 – $190,000 | $40,500 plus 37 cents for each $1 over $135,000 |
| $190,001 and over | $60,850 plus 45 cents for each $1 over $190,000 |

### Working Holiday Maker Rates

Working holiday makers (subclass 417 and 462 visas) pay:
- 15% on the first $45,000
- Standard rates on income above $45,000

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much tax do I pay on $100,000 in Australia?
On a $100,000 salary in FY2025-26, you pay $20,788 in income tax. That's an effective rate of 20.79%. Add the 2% Medicare levy ($2,000) and your total tax is $22,788. Your take-home pay is approximately $77,212 per year, or $1,484.85 per week.

### What is the highest tax rate in Australia?
The highest marginal tax rate is 45%, which applies to taxable income over $190,000. Including the 2% Medicare levy, the top marginal rate is effectively 47%. If you don't have private health insurance and earn over $144,000, the Medicare Levy Surcharge adds another 1.5%, bringing it to 48.5%.

### When do the new tax rates start?
The current FY2025-26 rates have been in effect since 1 July 2025. The next change is a reduction in the lowest bracket rate from 16% to 15%, taking effect from 1 July 2026.

### How is taxable income calculated?
Taxable income is your gross income minus allowable deductions. Deductions include work-related expenses (tools, uniforms, travel), self-education expenses, and professional memberships. Your employer withholds tax based on your gross pay through the PAYG system, and you reconcile any difference in your annual tax return.

### What's the difference between marginal rate and effective rate?
Your marginal rate is the tax rate on the last dollar you earn — it's the bracket your income falls into. Your effective rate is the average rate across all your income. For example, on $80,000 your marginal rate is 30% but your effective rate is only 18.49%.

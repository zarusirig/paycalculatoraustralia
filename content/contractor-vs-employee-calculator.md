## Key Differences at a Glance

| Factor | Employee | Contractor |
|--------|----------|-----------|
| Tax collection | Employer withholds (PAYG) | You lodge BAS quarterly |
| Super | Employer pays 12% on top | You pay your own (optional) |
| Medicare levy | ✅ Deducted from pay | ✅ You pay at tax time |
| GST | Not applicable | Must charge 10% if registered |
| Leave entitlements | 4 weeks annual + 10 days personal | None |
| Insurance | Employer provides workers comp | You arrange your own |
| Equipment | Usually employer-provided | Usually your own cost |

## When Does Contracting Pay Off?

Contracting can be worthwhile if you charge enough to cover the gap:

**Contracting is better when:**
- Your contract rate is 30%+ higher than the equivalent employee salary
- You can claim significant tax deductions (home office, travel, equipment)
- You enjoy the flexibility and are comfortable managing your own tax and super
- You have multiple clients (reducing risk)

**Employment is better when:**
- The pay rates are similar
- You value job security and leave entitlements
- You don't want to manage BAS, tax returns, and insurance yourself
- You need workers compensation coverage

## ATO Classification Rules

The ATO uses a multi-factor test to determine whether you're genuinely a contractor or actually an employee. Key factors include:

- **Control**: An employee works under direction; a contractor controls how the work is done
- **Independence**: Contractors typically work for multiple clients
- **Tools and equipment**: Contractors usually provide their own
- **Financial risk**: Contractors bear their own risk of loss
- **Ability to delegate**: Contractors can send a substitute

Getting the classification wrong has serious consequences — employers face back-payment of super, leave, and penalties.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Should I be a contractor or employee?
It depends on the rate differential. If contracting pays 30%+ more than the equivalent employee salary, the financial benefit usually outweighs the loss of entitlements. Below that, employment is typically better value. Use the calculator above to compare your specific scenario.

### Do contractors have to pay super?
Contractors are not legally required to pay their own super (unlike employers). However, for retirement planning, setting aside 12% voluntarily is strongly recommended. You can claim a tax deduction for personal super contributions.

### Do I need to register for GST as a contractor?
You must register for GST if your annual turnover is $75,000 or more. If below $75,000, registration is optional. When registered, you charge clients an additional 10% GST and remit it to the ATO quarterly via your BAS.

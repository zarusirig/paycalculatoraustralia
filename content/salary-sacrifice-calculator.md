## How Salary Sacrifice Works

Salary sacrifice redirects part of your gross salary to a benefit — most commonly superannuation — before income tax is calculated. Because you're taxed on a lower amount, you pay less income tax.

The trade-off: that money goes to your super fund (or another benefit), not your bank account. But the tax saving means you keep more of each dollar compared to receiving it as normal pay.

### The Basic Mechanics

| Step | Without Sacrifice ($100K) | With $10K Sacrifice ($100K) |
|------|------------------------|-----------------------------|
| Gross salary | $100,000 | $100,000 |
| Salary sacrifice | $0 | −$10,000 (to super) |
| Taxable income | $100,000 | $90,000 |
| Income tax | $20,788 | $17,788 |
| Medicare levy (2%) | $2,000 | $1,800 |
| **Net take-home** | **$77,212** | **$70,412** |
| Super contribution (employer 12%) | $12,000 | $12,000 |
| Extra super from sacrifice | $0 | $10,000 |
| **Total super** | **$12,000** | **$22,000** |
| Tax saved | — | **$3,200** |

You receive $6,800 less in take-home pay, but $10,000 goes to super — that's $3,200 in savings ($10,000 − $6,800). The money grows tax-effectively in your super fund at a concessional rate of 15% instead of your marginal rate.

## Things to Watch Out For

### Concessional Contribution Cap

Salary sacrifice counts toward your $30,000 annual concessional contribution cap (FY2025-26). This cap includes your employer's compulsory super contributions (12% SG).

On $100,000:
- Employer SG: $12,000
- Room for salary sacrifice: $30,000 − $12,000 = **$18,000**

If you exceed the cap, the excess is taxed at your marginal rate plus a charge — negating the benefit. [More on super caps →](/superannuation-guide/)

### Impact on Insurance and Loans

Reducing your taxable income through salary sacrifice can affect:
- **Home loan borrowing capacity** — lenders assess income after sacrifice
- **Income protection insurance** — may pay less if based on reduced salary
- **Government benefits** — some are means-tested on adjusted taxable income

### Salary Sacrifice Doesn't Reduce HECS

Reportable super contributions (including salary sacrifice) are added back to your income when calculating [HECS-HELP repayments](/hecs-help-calculator/). So salary sacrifice won't reduce your HECS repayment.

## Is Salary Sacrifice Worth It?

**Best for:**
- Middle-to-high income earners (30%+ marginal rate)
- People not close to retirement who benefit from long-term compounding
- Those who've already paid off high-interest debts

**Less useful for:**
- Low-income earners (16% marginal rate — minimal tax saving)
- Those with significant debt at rates above super returns
- Anyone who may need access to the money before preservation age (currently 60)

The general rule: salary sacrifice works well if your marginal tax rate is significantly higher than the 15% super contributions tax — that's anyone earning above $45,000.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much can I salary sacrifice?
There's no legal limit on the amount, but your total concessional super contributions (employer SG + salary sacrifice) can't exceed $30,000 per year without penalty. On $100,000, your employer contributes $12,000, leaving room for up to $18,000 in salary sacrifice.

### Does salary sacrifice reduce my take-home pay?
Yes — the amount you sacrifice is redirected to your super fund (or other benefit) before tax. But the tax saving means you lose less than the full sacrifice amount. Sacrificing $10,000 on an $80,000 salary reduces your take-home by only $6,800.

### Can I salary sacrifice into things other than super?
Yes. Salary sacrifice can also be used for novated leases (cars), additional employer super contributions, and some other items. However, non-super items may attract Fringe Benefits Tax (FBT), reducing the benefit. Super sacrifice is the most tax-effective option for most people.

### Should I salary sacrifice or pay off my mortgage?
If your mortgage rate is higher than your expected super return (after accounting for the tax advantage), prioritise the mortgage. At current rates, salary sacrifice into super typically offers a better after-tax return than extra mortgage repayments — but this depends on your specific rates.

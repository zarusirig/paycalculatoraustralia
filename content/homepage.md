## How the Pay Calculator Works

Your gross salary goes through several deductions before it becomes your take-home pay. Our calculator applies each one in the correct order using the official rates published by the Australian Taxation Office for the 2025-26 financial year.

Here's what we calculate:

- **Income tax** based on your [marginal tax rate](/tax-brackets/) — not a flat percentage, but the progressive rate that applies to each bracket of your income
- **Medicare levy** at 2% of your taxable income, plus the Medicare Levy Surcharge if applicable
- **HECS-HELP repayment** if you have a student debt and earn above the $67,000 threshold
- **Superannuation** at 12% — paid by your employer on top of your salary (shown for reference but doesn't reduce your take-home)

The result is your net pay — the amount that actually lands in your bank account each pay cycle.

## Australian Tax Brackets 2025-26

Your income tax is calculated in stages. Each portion of your income is taxed at a different rate — you don't pay the top rate on your entire salary.

| Taxable Income | Tax Rate |
|---------------|----------|
| $0 – $18,200 | Nil (tax-free) |
| $18,201 – $45,000 | 16c for each $1 over $18,200 |
| $45,001 – $135,000 | $4,288 + 30c for each $1 over $45,000 |
| $135,001 – $190,000 | $31,288 + 37c for each $1 over $135,000 |
| $190,001 and over | $51,638 + 45c for each $1 over $190,000 |

For example, on an $80,000 salary you pay $14,788 in income tax — that's an effective rate of 18.49%, not the 30% marginal rate that applies to your top bracket. The [Low Income Tax Offset (LITO)](/low-income-tax-offset/) can reduce this further for incomes under $66,667.

## What's Included in Your Pay Breakdown

Every line on your pay breakdown comes from an official government source. Here's what each component means:

### Income Tax
The amount withheld from your pay under the PAYG system, based on the [tax brackets above](/income-tax-calculator/). Your employer sends this directly to the ATO on your behalf each pay cycle.

### Medicare Levy
A flat 2% of your taxable income that funds Australia's public healthcare system. On $80,000, that's $1,600 per year. If you earn above $93,000 and don't have private hospital cover, you may also pay the [Medicare Levy Surcharge](/medicare-levy/) of 1% to 1.5%.

### HECS-HELP Repayment
If you have a university debt, compulsory repayments start when your income reaches $67,000 in FY2025-26. The new marginal system means you only pay on income above the threshold — not on your entire salary. [Calculate your exact HECS repayment →](/hecs-help-calculator/)

### Superannuation (12%)
Your employer pays 12% of your Ordinary Time Earnings into your super fund on top of your base salary. On an $80,000 salary, that's $9,600 per year. This appears on your breakdown but doesn't reduce your take-home pay. [Learn more about super →](/superannuation-calculator/)

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much tax do I pay on $80,000 in Australia?
On an $80,000 salary in FY2025-26, you pay $14,788 in income tax plus $1,600 in Medicare levy — a total of $16,388. Your take-home pay is approximately $63,612 per year, or $1,223.31 per week. Your employer also pays $9,600 into your super fund on top of your salary.

### What is the tax-free threshold in Australia?
The tax-free threshold is $18,200. You pay no income tax on the first $18,200 you earn. With the Low Income Tax Offset (LITO), the effective tax-free threshold increases to $22,575 — meaning some people earning up to this amount may pay no net tax at all.

### How much super does my employer pay?
From 1 July 2025, employers must pay 12% of your Ordinary Time Earnings into your super fund. On an $80,000 salary, that's $9,600 per year. This is paid on top of your salary — it doesn't reduce your take-home pay unless you also salary sacrifice.

### What is the Medicare levy?
The Medicare levy is 2% of your taxable income, collected to help fund Australia's public healthcare system. On $80,000, that's $1,600 per year. Low-income earners below approximately $27,222 may pay a reduced levy or be exempt entirely.

### How do HECS-HELP repayments work in 2025-26?
From FY2025-26, HECS-HELP uses a new marginal repayment system. Compulsory repayments start when your income reaches $67,000, and you only pay on the amount above the threshold — not on your total income. The rate is 15 cents per dollar over $67,000 for the first band ($67,001–$125,000).

### What's the difference between gross and net pay?
Gross pay is your total salary before any deductions. Net pay (or take-home pay) is what you actually receive after income tax, Medicare levy, and any HECS repayments are deducted. For example, $80,000 gross becomes approximately $63,612 net.

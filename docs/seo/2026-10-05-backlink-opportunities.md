## 4. Six to eight new linkable assets (all inside the topical border)

Each must also pass the three border rules in `contextual-borders-and-audience-map.md`. None involve invented data or personas.

1. **Australian tax and pay tables as open data (CSV + JSON + GitHub repo).** Resident tax rates, Medicare levy, super guarantee, HECS thresholds, minimum wage by year, FY by FY. Why it earns links: developers, accountants and spreadsheet users link to "the dataset"; GitHub READMEs and "awesome" lists give dofollow links, and competitors lack it. Aligns with the plan's note that tax tables are the top KD-0 target. Sources must be cited to ATO/FWC; keep it in sync on a schedule.
2. **Minimum Wage Dataset, 1997 to present, with by-age and award comparisons (CSV, charts, embeddable).** Why: every June (annual wage review) journalists, unions and NGOs need the history; AWU/Australian Unions pages draw links for this reason. Add a "cite this" box.
3. **"What changes and when" pay calendar (1 Dec, 1 Jan, 1 Jul, per-state dates) with a subscribable .ics and per-salary tax-cut table.** Why: Canstar and The Conversation run annual July explainers, and a single dated page is quoted. Extend the existing `/tax-calendar/` and `/tax-changes-2026-27/`. Confirm dates against FWC/ATO each time; per the project notes, the FWCFB junior change is a phase-in and not the adult rate on 1 Dec 2026.
4. **Live-updating "current figures" badges.** Tiny embeds (national minimum wage, super guarantee rate, tax-free threshold) that update when rates change. Why: HR and payroll blogs hate stale figures; a badge with a credit link is easy to adopt and stays correct without maintenance. Needs visible credit and a nofollow-friendly design.
5. **Quarterly Take-Home Pay Index.** After-tax pay at set salaries combined with ABS Average Weekly Earnings / Wage Price Index releases, published each quarter with a short press note. Why: a repeatable series gives journalists a reason to return and gives us a recurring peg. Start with what the pay report already computes.
6. **Public-sector and award pay comparison by state (APS, VPS, NSW teachers, nurses, police, ADF) with CSV.** Why: it answers a real recurring question, is hard to collect elsewhere, and unions and Reddit communities share it. The competitor gap analysis already flagged this cluster. State only published figures and cite the agreement.
7. **"Your tax cut" calculator for the 1 July 2026 and legislated 2027 cuts, with a shareable result.** Why: tax-cut stories recur in news, Treasury/ATO explainers are generic, and a per-salary calculator is what a journalist embeds or links.
8. **HECS-HELP explainer plus repayment chart for 2026-27** (page upgrade, not a new route). Why: the one proven news-link pattern for the competitor. Verify thresholds and the repayment method against the ATO before publishing.

Not recommended: a salary survey (we have no respondents and must not invent data), skyscraper "ultimate guides" for tax brackets (SERP does not need links), infographics for their own sake, and any guest-post marketplace or paid placement.
